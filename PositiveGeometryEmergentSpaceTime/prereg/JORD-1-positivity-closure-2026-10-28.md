**Definitions.** V = h3(O), basis 0–2 E1..E3; 3–10 a at (2,3); 11–18 b at (3,1); 19–26 c at (1,2) (1-based matrix positions); product scaled 2X∘Y = XY + YX. Octonions: Fano lines (1,2,4),(2,3,5),(3,4,6),(4,5,7),(5,6,1),(6,7,2),(7,1,3). f4 = Der(V) as 27×27 matrices. stab(S) = {D ∈ f4 : D s = 0 ∀ s ∈ S}; setstab(W) = {D : D W ⊆ W}; Fix(h) = common kernel; closure(h) = stab(Fix(h)). C = span(1,e1), H = span(1,e1,e2,e4); B = h3(C); X = h2(C) on positions {2,3}. **g_sm := stab(E1) ∩ setstab(B).**
**Galois lemma (PROVED on paper here; Lean form in §4).** For S ⊆ V, stab(Fix(stab S)) = stab S. Hence some S has stab(S) = g ⇔ closure(g) = g. Fix(g) is a Jordan subalgebra containing I, spanned by its idempotents (spectral theorem in a formally real Jordan algebra), and idempotents lie in the closed positive cone; stab of a cone point equals stab of its spectral idempotents. So "g is the pointwise stabiliser of a finite set of cone points / faces" ⇔ "g is closed". (Faces of the cone correspond to idempotents, so face stabilisers are included.)
**Non-uniqueness (rule 8).** The choice u = e1 inside Im O, the choice of the distinguished idempotent E1 among three, and the J-variant in the rival are conventions; V1/V2 test that the checker ACCEPTS equivalent conventions.
