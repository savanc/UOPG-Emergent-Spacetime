**Pattern detail** [SIMULATED]:
- R1a co-occurs only with T1 or T3, and R1c only with T2 or T4.
- R1b = T5 exactly.
- So R1a ⊂ T1 ∪ T3 and R1c ⊂ T2 ∪ T4, and their union misses the rest.
- The H-T5 n = 8 "neighbour cut" picture was a coincidence of small k: every neighbour-cut network at k = 4 is
  star–triangle equivalent to a tree. At k = 5, the cuts that keep two *adjacent* bonds produce loop networks, which are
  not tiles.

## Deviations (disclosed)

1. **Shape position.** The prereg asked for back-substitution in shape position. The raw lex basis was not in shape
   position, so a separating linear form st = c + λ₁a + λ₂b was added (standard; same certification).
2. **(c) orientation.** The prereg did not fix the orientation of a 3-fold residue. Each tile form is signed by its value
   at a generic interior point of that tile (a canonical form has no zeros or poles inside its tile).
   - The path Y(ε) = C(m* + εw)Λ used a generic w. A first attempt with inward directions only found positive
     directions for 3 of 5 walls.
   - The reported run uses one convention for all 5 walls.

## Do networks choose the tiling? (plain words)

**PARTIAL**, as pre-registered.
- The rule we extrapolated from n = 8 Ising data ("cut neighbouring boundary bonds") **fails** at n = 10: its tiles are
  disjoint but cover only about 80%.
- What works is "**the tiles are exactly the loop-free (tree) Ising networks**". These 5 cells tile the amplituhedron,
  every sample sits in exactly one, and the spurious poles cancel on every internal wall.
- That is a clean Ising-language statement: trees have no closed loops, so HWX's loop factor reduces to its V-face
  part, and the J1+J2 face terms are absent.
- But it is Perlstein–Tessler's "tree of triangles" theorem read in GP's dictionary, not something the Ising model
  derives on its own. Networks *name* the tiling cleanly; we have not shown that Ising physics *selects* it.
