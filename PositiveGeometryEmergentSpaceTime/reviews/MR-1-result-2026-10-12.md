## Plain words

The Lovelace–Shapiro amplitude has one input (the ρ mass) and two fixed numbers (the Adler zero and Regge
linearity). From those it predicts that the f2 couples to pions about half as strongly (squared) as the ρ, and that
the f2's mass squared is 3 times the ρ's.

Nature has the f2 coupling about a third larger than LS predicts, and its mass squared about 10 % smaller. Both are the
size of the expected 1/N_c corrections.

So LS passes, but only at the level of "large-N_c string-like model, ±30 %". It is not a sharp test.
