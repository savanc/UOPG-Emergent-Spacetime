## Plain language

The trace-order observer chain (H-T1) is an electrical network, and Lam's map puts every such network at a point of the
positive Grassmannian. This rung asked *which cell* it lands in, and whether that cell's canonical form looks like a
scattering amplitude.

- **The cell is the network's wiring diagram.** The positroid cell is read off from the network's medial pairing τ
  (how the "wires" between terminals cross). We confirmed this on 302 networks and proved two cases in Lean.
- **The network is a thin slice of its cell.** A network with c edges is a c-dimensional slice of a
  (2c + n − 1)-dimensional positroid cell. Any amplitude-style form living on the full cell therefore vanishes on the
  network.
- **The network's own form exists, but it is not an amplitude.** It is the dlog of conductances. Remarkably, it does
  not depend on which equivalent wiring you pick: the star–triangle move preserves it exactly, as square moves do for
  on-shell diagrams. But its boundary structure is nothing like an amplitude polytope. The 3-terminal space has only 3
  facets; a 3-dim associahedron has 9.
- **The preprint's decorated permutations are much coarser.** They only see which terminals can reach which. Every
  connected network gets the same label, whatever its wiring.

## Results with tags

**(a) Cells.** [SIMULATED]
- Families: all edge subsets of three supergraphs.
  - n = 2: edge + centre, 8 subgraphs.
  - n = 3: triangle + 3 spokes, 64.
  - n = 4: 4-cycle + 4 spokes, 256.
- Method, in exact rationals: random positive rational conductances; grove sums by forest enumeration; Δ_I by Lam's
  concordance (dual partitions via Kreweras); Grassmann necklace → bounded affine permutation f.
- For every subgraph with a point (6, 56, 240):
  - (1) g = f + 1 mod 2n is a fixed-point-free involution τ, and f = f_τ exactly (Lam §4.6).
  - (2) c(τ) = Jacobian rank of the grove ratios w.r.t. conductances (floats, SVD) = electroid dimension.
  - (3) k(2n−k) − ℓ(f) = n − 1 + 2c.
- Distinct cells reached: 2 of 3 (n = 2), 8 of 15 (n = 3), 52 of 105 (n = 4). The remaining strata are cactus
  networks (boundary points glued), which these families don't contain. [CITED: Lam Thm 4.9.]
- The general facts are CITED: Lam Thms 5.6, 5.8 and 4.16. This is an independent check of them, not a new theorem.

**(b) Lean.** [PROVED]
- `facet_cell` + `F_pluecker` + `F_pattern`, for the network "terminal 1 joined to 2 (b) and 3 (c)":
  - Exact 2×6 representative `MF b c`.
  - All 15 minors computed, with Δ₂₃ = Δ₅₆ = 0 and the other 13 > 0.
  - Necklace (12, 24, 34, 45, 15, 16).
  - f = [4,3,5,7,6,8] = Lam's f_τ for τ = (15)(24)(36), c(τ) = 2 = edges, positroid dimension 6 = 2 + 2·2.
- `top_cell`: the all-positive Y point (H-T1 `Y_pos`) gives f = [3,…,8] = f_{τ_top}, c = 3, dim 8.
- `dim_formula_n3`: dim Π_{f_τ} = 2 + 2c(τ) for all 15 matchings of [6].
- `crossing_census_n3`: stratum counts 5/6/3/1 of E₃.
- `yd_bijOn`: the star–triangle map is a bijection ℝ³_{>0} → ℝ³_{>0}, so the top electroid cell has dimension 3 = the
  number of edges of either critical network.
- All combinatorics is by `decide` (kernel), not `native_decide`.

**(c) Canonical form.**
- **n = 2** [DERIVED]: E₂ is a segment, [x : y] ≥ 0 with effective conductance x/y ∈ [0, ∞]. Its form dlog(x/y) has
  two poles (open and short circuit). Every segment has this form, including the 4-point bi-adjoint 1/X + 1/(C−X), so
  the match is trivial.
- **Star–triangle preserves dlog** [PROVED, `star_triangle_dlog`]:
  - det ∂(log x₁₂, log x₁₃, log x₂₃)/∂(log a, log b, log c) = −1.
  - The two derivative shapes are checked with `HasDerivAt` (`logJac_entry00/20`). The other 7 entries follow by
    symmetry from the same lemmas; they were checked by sympy, not each in Lean.
  - So the conductance dlog form ω_τ is independent of the critical representative, up to orientation.
  - **This falsifies my pre-registered sub-claim** that the Y pull-back would differ. The pole at a = 0 in the Y chart
    is the exceptional divisor of a blow-down onto x₁₂ = x₁₃ = 0, which a dlog form tolerates.
  - Novelty: probably known in spirit (cluster mutations preserve the log-volume form, and square-move invariance of
    on-shell forms is CITED, ABCGPT). I found no statement of it for electrical Y–Δ, so it is not claimed as new.
- **Candidate Ω₃** [DERIVED; status CONJECTURE]:
  - Ω₃ = Res_Q ⟨L d⁴L⟩/(Q · L₁₂|₃ L₁₃|₂ L₂₃|₁) on the grove quadric Q: L₁₂₃L₁|₂|₃ = e₂ (`grove_quadric` PROVED on the
    Y chart).
  - In charts it equals dlog x₁₂ ∧ dlog x₁₃ ∧ dlog x₂₃, and it has no poles at L₁₂₃ = 0 or L₁|₂|₃ = 0 (sympy, chart
    L₁₂|₃ = 1).
  - Its 3 polar divisors are exactly the 3 facets. Each facet is a smooth quadric surface ≅ ℙ¹×ℙ¹ whose positive part
    is a square, i.e. E₂ × E₂ (the two 2-terminal halves glued at terminal 1). The residue there is dlog x ∧ dlog y.
  - Q is smooth.
  - This is a concrete candidate for the n = 3 case of Lam's **open** conjecture that E_n is a positive geometry
    [CITED as open: Lam, *Invitation*, Remark 4]. We did not verify every axiom, e.g. that E₃'s semialgebraic
    boundary is exactly these divisors' positive parts. So it stays CONJECTURE.
- **Amplitude comparison** [DERIVED from Lam's stratification + `scripts/ht2_canonical.py`]:
  - E₃ has f-vector (V, E, F) = (5, 6, 3). A convex 3-polytope needs ≥ 4 facets, so E₃ is no polytope and in
    particular not K₅ = (14, 21, 9).
  - E₄ (dim 6) has 4 facets, against 27 for the 6-dim associahedron.
  - The form has a single monomial, with poles where an effective conductance vanishes, and no sum over channels.
  - Pre-set rule: **FAIL (only combinatorial).**
  - Noted without moving the target: facets factorise as products of lower network spaces (E₂ × E₂) with product
    residues. That is the *structural* pattern of locality/unitarity in positive geometries, but with no kinematics
    attached. On-shell N=4 cell forms are also single dlog monomials; what makes them amplitudes is the kinematic
    δ-functions, which networks lack. [SPECULATION about what could supply them: next rungs.]

**(d) *Fusions*.** [SIMULATED on 302 networks; PROVED at n = 3 for the Y and facet networks]
- `terminal_minors`: the terminal-column minors all equal L_{1…n}, the spanning-tree sum.
- `fusions_matches_projection`: so for a connected network the projection to the terminal columns sits in the top
  cell of Gr(n−1, n), f = a + n − 1. That equals *Fusions* Def. 2 applied to the (irreducible) trace chain.
- For disconnected networks the projection is degenerate (zero), while *Fusions* lists the components.
- On the families, *Fusions*' label is a **function of** Lam's cell (one label per cell), with 2 → 2, 8 → 5 and
  52 → 14 labels. Among connected networks, 1, 4 and 27 cells all map to the single *Fusions* label.
- *Fusions* applied to the full chain (interior nodes included) is the top cell of Gr(N−1, N) for any connected
  network, which is even less informative.
- **Conclusion:** the two permutations agree only on connectivity. Lam's τ carries the wiring; *Fusions* forgets it
  (in line with essay §4 finding 1).

## Honest caveats

1. The families miss cactus strata (half of E₄). Lam's general theorem covers them, but we didn't test them.
2. Jacobian ranks are floating-point (SVD). Plücker data and permutations are exact.
3. Ω₃ is a candidate, not a proved canonical form (it is part of an open conjecture). The n = 4 form is not computed.
4. The (c) verdict depends on the pre-set meaning of "amplitude-like". A looser reading (factorising boundaries) would
   call it suggestive. I kept the pre-set rule.
5. Of the log-Jacobian, 2 of 9 entries were derivative-checked in Lean; the other 7 were checked by symmetry and sympy.
6. The re-read gap is disclosed in the prereg: (i)–(iv) were derived by hand before computing, so (a), (b) and (d)
   confirm expectations. Only (c)'s sub-claim was a genuine test, and it came out wrong.
