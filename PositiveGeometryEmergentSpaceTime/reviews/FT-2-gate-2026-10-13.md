# FT-2: unequal-mass hidden zeros at 6 points (LO ChPT, SU(3)-broken). 2026-10-13

## Question
CGW 2601.16860 shows that massive NLSM hidden zeros survive for a *uniform* spurion mass (M = m²·1) and leaves non-uniform spurions open. Does LO ChPT with unequal quark masses (π, K, η sector) keep the 6-point zeros?

## Prior art [CITED]
- CGW 2601.16860 (PRD): the zeros survive only for symmetry-controlled mass generation. A naive pion mass term spoils them, while a spurion potential restores them. They treat the uniform case; the non-uniform case is explicitly open.
- Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024 (massless NLSM zeros); Rodina 2406.04234; Feng–Zhang–Zhou 2504.14215 and the JHEP 01(2026)010 hidden-zero recursion are all massless or uniform-mass.
- Bijnens et al. 2107.06291: the six-pion amplitude in O(N+1)/O(N) only. They call SU(Nf) six-meson amplitudes future work.
- I found no paper treating unequal-mass 6-point zeros, so this is not credited elsewhere as far as the sweep shows.

## Gate
- **Method fitness: PASS.** I used exact rational arithmetic, so a zero is an exact 0, not a small number. The flavour-ordered tree amplitudes come from Berends–Giele recursion in the U(3) quark-line basis, with L = F²/4 tr ∂U∂U† + F²B/2 tr M(U+U†) and M = diag(m_u, m_d, m_s) unequal.
- **Well-posedness: PASS.** The candidate loci come from Lean `mesh_shift_cancels`: in the quark-line basis the unequal mass shifts cancel identically in the mesh variables, so the shifted c̃ equals −2 p_a·p_b. The candidate loci are therefore p_a·p_b = 0 on mesh rectangles. No parameters are involved.

## Validation [SIMULATED]
- The exponential and Cayley parametrisations agree exactly on-shell for every point tested.
- The massless limit reproduces the known zeros.
- Non-rectangle sets ({(0,2),(0,3),(1,3)} and a single pair) are non-zero: these are the null controls.
- Negative control: with a naive mass term (only the quadratic mass term, no spurion tower), the zeros are spoiled, which reproduces CGW's finding. This shows the test can fail.

## Result [SIMULATED, exact arithmetic]
- At 4 points, the zero at p0·p2 = 0 holds for every flavour chain with μ = (1, 2, 7).
- At 6 points, the skinny rectangle {p0·p2, p0·p3, p0·p4 = 0} and the 2×2 rectangle {p0·p3, p0·p4, p1·p3, p1·p4 = 0} give an exact 0. This holds for 5 flavour chains (udsuds, uuddss, ududud, ususus, uuuuus) × 3 mass sets (equal, unequal 1:2:7, massless), with 2 random rational kinematic points each.
- **So the CGW hidden zeros survive SU(3)-broken, unequal-mass LO ChPT at 6 points.** The status is SIMULATED, not PROVED: the all-n statement is a CONJECTURE. The algebraic cancellation that defines the loci is PROVED in Lean (`FT2Mesh`, 0 sorry, standard axioms only).
- The code and outputs are in sims/ft2/ (ft2.py, run.py, control.py, *_output.txt).

## Caveats
- These are flavour-ordered, quark-line (U(3)) amplitudes. Physical π⁰, η, η′ are mixtures, and there is no U(1)_A anomaly at LO. Full amplitudes are sums over orderings, and a full-amplitude zero needs every contributing ordering to vanish on the same locus. Which physical 6-meson channels satisfy that is a CONJECTURE that has not been checked.
- Tree level only. I did not test the 5-point odd case (NLSM+φ³) or the 2610.01775 splitting zeros (factorisation near the zero).

## Observable / blind target: NO-GO [PROVED]
- Lean `FT2Mesh.zero_unphysical` (which reuses MR-6 `DecayZeroNoGo.no_zero`) shows that for massive physical kinematics every pair has p·p ≥ m m (same side) or ≤ −m m (crossed). So the loci p_a·p_b = 0 never lie in any 3→3, 2→4 or 1→5 physical region.
- Three-particle finite-volume lattice studies extract threshold K-matrix data, not values at these complex or unphysical points. So there is no direct blind target.
- No values were read.
