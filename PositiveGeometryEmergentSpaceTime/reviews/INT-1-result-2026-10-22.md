## Steelman of the opposing view
"Integers from positive geometry can pin ratios": (a) the canonical form/amplitude, not the face lattice, carries the physics, and residues at boundaries could quantise something; (b) imposing integrality of c′ (a lattice of kinematic data) plus extra positivity data (e.g. lattice-point counts = given integers) does cut μ to finitely many values — I_lat is cell-constant, so prescribing I_lat AND I_comb restricts μ to a bounded region, and at fixed small integers to finitely many rationals; (c) at higher n or with loop-level/surfaceology data the face lattice may stop being generic. Reply: (a) residues are ±1 on every facet in every chamber (combinatorial; CITED 1711.09102), carry no ratio; (b) that is an external lattice + chosen integers — discreteness imported, not derived, and the allowed μ set still has many members; (c) remains open (CONJECTURE / reopen trigger).

## What was learned for the mission gaps
- **Discreteness gap:** the associahedron's integers are locally constant on an open chamber, so they cannot by themselves fix any continuous dimensionless ratio; discreteness must come from an external datum (a lattice of c′, flux, modular weight, Ehrhart count). P1 closed. PROVED (n=5) / SIMULATED (n=6).
- **Scale gap:** untouched — c′ is dimensionful; only ratios are free and only walls (codim-1 mass relations such as m²_X35 = c24 − m²_X24 + m²_X25) are special. A wall is a *relation*, not a value. SPECULATION: a dynamical mechanism selecting "on-wall" kinematics would turn walls into mass relations; nothing here supplies it.
