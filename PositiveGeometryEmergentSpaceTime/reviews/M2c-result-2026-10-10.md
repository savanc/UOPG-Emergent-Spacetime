## 3. Proved vs cited vs hypothesised

- **PROVED (Lean):** for n = 5 with explicit coordinates — the pentagon's combinatorics, the identity
  "canonical form of the polygon = sum over planar cubic diagrams", factorisation on every facet,
  the soft-limit zero, and the role of c_ij > 0.
- **CITED:** that this object *is* the kinematic space of massless 5-point scattering and that
  m₅ is the bi-adjoint φ³ amplitude (ABHY §2–3); that the planar scattering form pulls back to the
  canonical form (§3.3); the general-n theorem (A_n is an associahedron, vertices ↔ triangulations).
  The A₆ counts (14 vertices, 9 facets) are proved only *combinatorially* (triangulations, diagonals);
  the geometric correspondence is cited.
- **Not used as a hypothesis:** no physical input beyond the definitions — M2c is pure geometry plus
  a cited identification of what the geometry means.
- **Definition choice:** "canonical form" is our fan-triangulation formula; uniqueness of canonical
  forms (Arkani-Hamed–Bai–Lam 1703.04541) is cited, and we cross-checked two different fans and ABHY's
  triangulation numerically.

## 4. What this shows (and doesn't)

Shows, for a toy scalar theory (tree-level bi-adjoint φ³, n = 5): a polytope defined only by
positivity (X ≥ 0) on a slice fixed by positive constants produces the full amplitude. **Locality**
(poles exactly at X = 0) is the boundary list; **unitarity/factorisation** (residue = lower amplitude)
is the boundary-of-boundary structure. Positivity does work: flip one sign of c and a facet, a pole
and the identity disappear; shrink the c's and the amplitude vanishes.

Does not show: anything about the Standard Model, gauge theory, masses, loops, or data. φ³ is a
toy; this is the cleanest known instance of "physics from positive geometry", now machine-checked.

Link to M2/M2b: there, Im A ≥ 0 (unitarity) made a *measure* positive and the EFT-hedron's faces
followed. Here the same unitarity statement appears as factorisation on facets — residues are
positive-coefficient lower amplitudes. Same principle, two geometries (moment curve vs polytope).
