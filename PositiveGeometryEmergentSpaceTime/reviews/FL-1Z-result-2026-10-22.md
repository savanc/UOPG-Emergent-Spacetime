## Model (fixed in the prereg, before any output)
The model is the Zee–Babu neutrino model with non-holomorphic modular A4 from arXiv:2502.12662 (Kobayashi–Okada–Orikasa), CITED (fetched this run).
- **Fields and weights:** L_L ~ 3 (weight −1), ℓ_R ~ 3 (+1), S⁻ ~ 1 (+2), k⁺⁺ ~ 1 (0).
- **Mass matrices:** M_ℓ = a_e P + b_e S(Y0) + c_e A(Y0); f = A(Y0); g = S(Y−2) + b̃_k P; m_ν = f M_ℓ* g M_ℓ† fᵀ.
- **Forms:** App. A q-series, truncated at n ≤ 4.
- **Target:** ρ = (m2/m3)² = Δm²sol/Δm²atm. The NuFit 3σ band is [0.02655, 0.03268].

## Results (each claim tagged)
**Why the rung is not well-posed (§0 reasons, each confirmed):**
- The model needs fitted parameters: a 2-real charged-lepton fit, plus b̃_k ∈ ℂ, plus δ = τ − ω. The published best fit has Δχ² ≈ 16, with s12² outside 3σ. CITED(fetched 2502.12662 Tab. II).
- The exact fixed point is excluded, both by L2 and in the paper. PROVED (`D3_rank_one`) and SIMULATED(r3).
- The ε-independent output m1 = 0 is Zee–Babu-generic. PROVED (`antisymm3_det_zero`, `antisymm3_kernel`) and SIMULATED(r3).

**Planted tests:**
- **L1 (fixed-point forms) PASS.** My App. A transcription gives Y3^(0)(ω) = b0·v1 with b0 = −0.262274, and Y3^(−2)(ω) = b−2·v_ω with b−2 = 0.416619. The relative residuals are 1.0e-10 and 1.9e-8; the paper's values are −0.2623 and 0.4166. SIMULATED(r3, identical in r1 and r2).
- **L2 (published τ = ω exclusion, 2502.12662 App. D) PASS.** Over 20 random real (a_e, b_e, c_e), m_ν has max m2/m3 = 3.3e-15, so it is rank 1 with two massless neutrinos. Its overlap with the eq. - **L3 (m1 = 0 for every τ) PASS.** Max m1/m3 = 1.5e-16 over 50 random points. SIMULATED(r3).

## Steelman of the opposing view
"Modular fixed points do give fit-free structure." Near τ = ω the residual Z3 symmetry forces mass hierarchies that scale as |ε|^k, and k is an *integer* fixed by representations and weights (the Novichkov–Penedo–Petcov–Titov / Feruglio programme; not fetched this run, so not CITED). On that view the prediction is the exponent k, and the hierarchies, like m_e/m_τ, are "explained" by a single small ε rather than by fitted Yukawas. The 2502.12662 exclusion also shows that the fixed point is *predictive*: it rules out the exact point and NH-only survives.

Reply:
- The exponent is not a dimensionless observable by itself. ρ = c·|ε|^k still needs the free ε and an O(1) coefficient that depends on the branch.
- In this model the post-hoc slopes do not show a clean integer (median 1.9, wide spread).
- NH comes from Zee–Babu (m1 = 0), not from the fixed point.

The exponent route is the strongest survivor. It would need its own prereg (proposed below as FL-1Zb), with branch tracking and a model where the exponent is derived and not fitted.

## What was learned for the mission gaps
- **Discreteness gap.**
  - The modular weights are integers, and the valence formula forces low-weight zeros onto τ = i or ω (`valence_lt12`, PROVED under `hv`). That is a genuine discrete selection of *points*.
  - But the viable phenomenology sits at a free distance from those points, and every observable varies with that distance and with b̃_k. SIMULATED(r3).
  - So the discreteness of the weights does not pass through to a dimensionless ratio. As with INT-1, the integers select a locus (a wall or a fixed point), not a value.
  - The only candidate discrete output is the hierarchy exponent k (CONJECTURE; not clean here).
- **Scale gap.** Untouched. κ (overall scale) drops out, and only ratios were studied. The fixed-point distance |ε| plays the role of a new free continuous parameter, a "scale" in modulus space. SPECULATION: a dynamical modulus-stabilisation mechanism fixing |ε| would be needed, and it would itself import a scale.
- **Combined lesson (INT-1 + FL-1Z).** Both "integer" mechanisms examined today fix a special codim-≥1 locus but leave the deviation from it free. This is a pattern, not a theorem: CONJECTURE "integers pick loci, not values".
