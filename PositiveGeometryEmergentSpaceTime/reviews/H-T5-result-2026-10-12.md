Prereg: `prereg/H-T5-ising-tiling-2026-10-12.md` (commit 2de6a6a, pushed before any computation).
Script and output: `scripts/ht5_tiling.py`, `scripts/ht5_tiling_output.txt`, `scripts/ht5_tiling.json`.

**Deviation (disclosed).** scipy could not be installed: the venv's pip is broken. `least_squares` was replaced by a
small numpy Levenberg–Marquardt with the same residual, tolerance (1e-10) and J box (1e-4, 20). After the prereg run, I
added a **post-hoc** retry: 100 starts, wider box |log J| < 12, applied to every negative entry. It is labelled
post-hoc and does not change any verdict.

## Reading of (b) [CONJECTURE]

Every sample lies in exactly one cell of {σ1, σ2} or in neither, never in both. The same holds for the cyclic pair.
The 0- and 1-count rows look like solver false negatives, not true gaps:
- The orthogonal momentum amplituhedron (OMA) region is known to be covered by HKWZ/HW.
- The misses cluster: all 26 all-zero rows fail for all four cells.

This is consistent with the published statement that each BCFW pair tiles. It does not prove it here. I suspect two
causes, both untested:
- some sampled Y have preimages at J outside the box, near a boundary;
- a branch issue: a solution may land on the other OG component, which the positive-J parametrisation cannot reach.

## (d) in plain words

The prereg's Ising-native rule ("flip one Ising coupling to zero vs to infinity") does **not** produce the tiling.
Post-hoc, I noticed a pattern that should be **pre-registered next** rather than claimed [SPECULATION]:
- The HW tiles σ1 and σ2 are both deletions (J → 0) of adjacent boundary edges (1,2) and (2,3) of the same network N1.
- The cyclic pair is N1 with (4,c) → ∞ vs (2,c) → 0.

So far, the tiling is only visible by importing the BCFW cells by hand. Ising data names the cells (GP, trivially),
but on this test it does not *select* the tiling.

## Novelty

- n = 6 is trivial: one cell (HKWZ).
- The n = 8 BCFW tiling is HKWZ/HW.
- The fact that every cell is an Ising cell is GP.

Nothing in H-T5 is a new mathematical result. The new items are:
- a formal (Lean) exact Ising point inside the n = 6 OMA with its sign-flip certificate (a small check of known facts);
- a negative result: the simplest Ising-native selection rule fails at n = 8.

## Caveats

- The membership solver is home-made (scipy was unavailable), so its false-negative rate is unknown. This is the main
  reason (b) is INCONCLUSIVE rather than PASS.
- Samples were drawn from the N1 top-cell image only.
- (c) was not run.
